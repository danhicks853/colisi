# Vision Pitch

## Capsule line
A cozy, never-punishing life sim where you are the magic that brings people together.
## Paragraph pitch
in this cozy life sim, you play as a magical wisp that teaches a small village how to tap into their latent magic. The central gameplay loop is twofold: day to day, you will be teaching various villagers how to use their magic to achieve their own goals. At big story moments, you will use your own magic to bring people together in friendship or love. The arc you're playing is individual character development causing an entire village to come together. This game is about evolving relationships in a world that never punishes you.
## Elevator pitch
You play as a small wisp who has been tasked to help a small village unlock their latent magic, by being a mentor, guide, and matchmaker in this cozy life sim. You will help the baker conjure a magical rolling pin, the carpenter drive the nail with his mind, and then create the perfect meet-cute by "accidentally" knocking over that iced coffee. 
Play on your time, with no pressure. You're a magical wisp, you don't need to sleep, or eat, or maintain your energy. There's always something to do and someone to help in this village, and your choices matter. It's Baldur's Gate III's branching dialog meets the Sims.
We want to bring a cozy gaming experience to the players who we *know* are out there that feel alienated by steep difficulty curves, content cliffs, and harsh punishments for not meeting arbitrary time limits.  It's a game for everyone who loves to improve the lives of others, at their own pace.
## 2–3 alternative-premise sketches

### Alternative A
An alternative was considered where the player would go through day/night cycles and be challenged to solve 2-4 puzzles before the day completed. While this would have driven daily play and goal-based completion, we felt this would generate anxiety in the player, and not satisfaction and relaxation. 
### Alternative B
We also considered a human main character that would build personal relationships with all of the villagers, but this felt too close to Stardew Valley. A human main character was appealing because of player-placeholder psychology, but precluded us from exploring private moments in villagers' lives, per-villager training sequences, and matchmaking mini-games. This also eliminated a source of "FOMO" where the player would have to commit to a relationship with one NPC while forsaking a relationship with another.
