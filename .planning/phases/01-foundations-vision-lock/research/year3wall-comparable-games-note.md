## Sources consulted

### 1. Chris Zukowski / howtomarketagame.com

Chris Zukowski is the most-cited indie marketing analyst for wishlist trajectories. Key public guidance (widely cited in indie dev community):

- **The "7,000 wishlists at launch" benchmark** — Zukowski's frequently-cited rule: 7,000 wishlists at Steam launch is the rough threshold below which an indie game struggles commercially; above which a game has reasonable commercial potential. This benchmark assumes ~10-20% conversion rate from wishlist to purchase in first 1-2 months post-launch + Steam algorithm favoring titles with stronger wishlist counts.
- **Cozy game category outperforms average** — cozy / wholesome / life-sim category historically converts wishlists to purchases at higher-than-average rates (community appetite is strong; the "underserved demographic" thesis aligns).
- **Steam Next Fest can produce 2-5x wishlist gains in days** — Next Fest participation drives substantial wishlist spikes for well-positioned indies (cozy games especially).
- **Wholesome Direct submissions** can produce similar magnitude wishlist boosts (specifically for cozy/wholesome titles).
- **Pre-launch wishlist trajectory generally**: most successful indies start wishlist accumulation 12-18 months pre-launch via Steam page, accumulate via devlog/Twitter/community, and see major spikes from festival participation in the final 6-12 months.

### 3. Solo-dev wishlist trajectory baseline

For solo developers in cozy genre without studio team or major publisher push, public data suggests:

- **First 6 months of Steam page (pre-Next-Fest / pre-Wholesome-Direct)**: 100-1000 wishlists typical for solo cozy indies with active devlog. Strong devlog presence can push higher.
- **Post-Next-Fest / Wholesome-Direct breakout**: 2-10k wishlist range possible for solo cozy with good festival placement.
- **Pre-launch (final 6 months)**: 5-20k wishlist range for solo cozy with festival participation + Steam algorithm momentum (varies wildly).
- **At launch**: 7-20k+ wishlist range for solo cozy titles considered commercially viable. <2-3k wishlists at launch generally correlates with commercial difficulty.

### 4. Productivity benchmarks for solo dev cozy projects

Less publicly-quantified data, but from public dev statements + indie dev community discussion:

- **Stardew Valley**: Eric Barone — 4+ years solo development (started 2012, launched 2016). Famously prolonged scope; outlier on duration.
- **Eastward** (Pixpil): ~5 years solo-leading development. Not pure cozy but adjacent.
- **Solo cozy indie average**: roughly 3-5 years from project start to commercial launch when developer is committed full-time and scope is well-bounded.
- **Solo cozy indie with AI assistance**: less data (newer pattern). AI assistance is widely-cited as accelerating boilerplate / engine work but not creative authoring. Daniel's project profile (solo + AI partner + 22-32 month M1 estimate) is on the more aggressive side of solo-cozy timelines.

- **Wishlist count**: conservative threshold 2,000-3,000 wishlists at m36; aggressive 5,000-10,000. **Below threshold → "Vol. 1 pivot" criteria fire.** Daniel-recommended starting position: 3,000 wishlists for trip (below 3k AND slice not shipped → strongly consider Vol. 1 pivot to shorter 15-20hr scope).

Recommended formula: "if slice has not shipped by m36 AND wishlist <3,000, formally evaluate Vol. 1 pivot (cut full-game scope to 15-20 hr v1 commercial launch, defer remaining 10-15+ hours to free post-launch updates)."
- **Productivity rate vs full-game scope**: at m36, evaluate observed productivity rate. If at observed pace, 30+ hour full game would take 24+ more months → consider Vol. 1 pivot.

### Productivity-delta threshold rationale (D-WALL-06)

Cumulative phase-completion delta is the productivity metric. Recommended interpretation:

- **m6**: roadmap estimates Phase 1 = 4-8 months + Phase 2 ~3 months. By m6, plan completion: ~Phase 1 done + Phase 2 mid. Delta computed as: months actually-elapsed minus months-of-roadmap-estimated-completion-achieved.
- **m12**: same calculation applied to roadmap cumulative estimate at m12 (Phase 3 most of way through).
- **m24**: cumulative estimate at m24 = ~Phase 6 mid (slice content production). 
- **m36**: cumulative estimate at m36 = slice shipped + Steam page live + Next Fest cycle complete.

Threshold ranges (months behind):
- m6 trip threshold: 2-3 months behind (Daniel decides; aggressive = 2; conservative = 3)
- m12 trip threshold: 3-4 months behind (Daniel decides)
- m24 trip threshold: 6-9 months behind (Daniel decides; this is approaching Year-3-Wall warning territory)
- m36 trip threshold: at the existential checkpoint, productivity-delta is one of several factors triggering Vol. 1 pivot evaluation
